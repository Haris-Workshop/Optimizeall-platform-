Passing the PMP is not the end of the story. To keep the credential active, you need to earn professional development units (PDUs) and renew it every three years. Many holders leave this until the last few months and then scramble for hours. It does not need to be like that: with a simple plan, the PDUs come naturally from learning you would want to do anyway.

This guide explains how **PMP PDUs** and renewal work under PMI's Continuing Certification Requirements (CCR), how to plan them across the cycle and how to record them so renewal is quick. PMI updates its rules from time to time, so check the current CCR handbook on PMI's website for the authoritative details.

For anything not covered here, the official guidance in PMI's CCR handbook is the reference.

## The essentials

As PMI describes the requirements at the time of writing:

- PMP holders need **60 PDUs** in each **three-year** certification cycle.
- PDUs fall into two categories: **Education** and **Giving Back**.
- At least **35 PDUs** must come from Education, including at least **8 PDUs** in each of the three Talent Triangle areas: **Ways of Working**, **Power Skills** and **Business Acumen**.
- Giving Back can contribute **up to 25 PDUs**. It is optional; you can earn all 60 from Education.
- One PDU generally corresponds to one hour of qualifying activity.

When you have earned and recorded the PDUs, you complete the renewal application and pay the renewal fee before your cycle ends.

## The Talent Triangle explained

PMI groups education into three skill areas:

| Area | What it covers | Example activities |
|---|---|---|
| Ways of Working | Methods and techniques for delivering projects: predictive, agile, hybrid, scheduling, risk, scope | A course on hybrid delivery or earned value |
| Power Skills | Leadership, communication, collaboration, emotional intelligence, conflict resolution | A negotiation workshop or a course on leading teams |
| Business Acumen | Strategy, benefits, finance, compliance, industry knowledge, organisational context | A course on business cases, project finance or AI governance |

The minimums ensure you keep developing across the whole profile rather than only in technical methods.

## Education PDUs

Education PDUs come from structured learning such as:

- courses and webinars (online or in person);
- conferences and chapter events with learning content;
- university or college courses;
- self-directed learning such as reading, podcasts or videos on relevant topics, within the limits PMI sets;
- organisational training that relates to project management.

Keep evidence of each activity: the provider, title, date, duration and a short description of what you learned.

## Giving Back PDUs

Giving Back recognises contributions to the profession, such as:

- **working as a practitioner** — PMI allows a limited number of PDUs for working in project management during the cycle;
- **creating content** — writing articles, presenting at events, producing educational material;
- **volunteering** — for PMI chapters or other organisations, in project-related roles;
- **mentoring** — sharing knowledge with other practitioners.

Each subcategory may have its own limits. Check the current CCR handbook before relying on a particular activity.

## A three-year plan that removes the panic

Spread the 60 PDUs evenly — roughly 20 a year, or under two a month.

### Year 1: build the foundation (about 20 PDUs)

- 8 PDUs Ways of Working: a course on a method you use less often, such as agile scaling or earned value.
- 6 PDUs Power Skills: a communication or leadership course.
- 6 PDUs Business Acumen: a course on benefits realisation, finance or your industry.

### Year 2: deepen and give back (about 20 PDUs)

- Complete any remaining Talent Triangle minimums.
- Add Giving Back: mentor a junior colleague, present a lessons-learned session at a chapter event, or volunteer.

### Year 3: top up and renew early (about 20 PDUs)

- Fill any gaps against the minimums.
- Aim to have all 60 PDUs recorded at least three months before your cycle ends.
- Submit the renewal well before the deadline.

## How to record PDUs without pain

1. **Record as you go.** Log each activity within a week while details are fresh.
2. **Keep evidence in one folder** — certificates, agendas, receipts, notes.
3. **Use clear descriptions.** "Completed eight-hour course on hybrid project delivery covering backlog management and stage gates" is better than "training".
4. **Check categories.** Assign each activity to the correct Talent Triangle area.
5. **Watch the limits.** Track Giving Back totals and any subcategory caps.

PMI may audit PDU claims, so accurate records matter.

## A sample PDU log

A simple spreadsheet is all you need. For example:

| Date | Activity | Provider | Hours | Category | Talent Triangle area | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Feb | Online course: hybrid delivery | Training provider | 8 | Education | Ways of Working | Certificate |
| Apr | Workshop: difficult conversations | Employer academy | 4 | Education | Power Skills | Attendance record |
| Jun | Webinar series: benefits management | Professional association | 3 | Education | Business Acumen | Registration emails |
| Sep | Mentoring a junior colleague | Self | 6 | Giving Back | n/a | Mentoring notes |

Add a running total by category and Talent Triangle area at the bottom. When you sit down to claim PDUs, you simply copy the entries across.

## Common renewal mistakes

- **Leaving it all to the final months.** Courses fill up, evidence is lost and the claim becomes stressful.
- **Missing a Talent Triangle minimum.** Having 60 PDUs is not enough if one area is short of its minimum.
- **Over-claiming Giving Back.** Hours above the cap do not count towards the total.
- **Vague descriptions.** Make each claim specific enough that someone else could see why it qualifies.
- **Forgetting the renewal step itself.** Earning PDUs does not renew the credential automatically; you still need to complete the renewal before the deadline.

## Using PDUs strategically

PDUs are an opportunity, not just an obligation. Use the cycle to build skills that matter for your next role:

- **AI and data.** Many organisations now expect project professionals to use AI responsibly. Courses on AI-assisted analysis, governance and prompt design build Ways of Working and Business Acumen.
- **Project controls.** If you want to move towards controls or PMO leadership, study scheduling, earned value and forecasting. Our comparison of [project controls certifications](/blog/project-controls-certifications-compared) shows where this can lead.
- **Finance.** Understanding business cases, cash flow and funding strengthens Business Acumen.

## Turning PDUs into a development plan

At the start of each cycle, write down one capability you want to have by the end of it — for example, "lead a hybrid programme with confidence" or "present forecasts to a board". Choose most of your Education PDUs to build that capability. Renewal then becomes a by-product of a deliberate development plan rather than a box-ticking exercise.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many PDUs do I need to renew the PMP?

PMI requires 60 PDUs every three years, with at least 35 from Education and at least 8 in each Talent Triangle area. Check PMI's current handbook for any changes.

### Can I carry PDUs over to my next cycle?

PMI has allowed PDUs earned in the final year of a cycle, beyond the 60 needed, to be carried over within limits. Check the current rules before planning on it.

### Do I have to earn Giving Back PDUs?

No. Giving Back is optional and capped. You can earn all 60 PDUs through Education.

### What happens if I do not renew on time?

PMI's handbook explains the process and any grace or suspension period. Avoid relying on it: plan to complete renewal before your cycle ends.

### Can my employer's training count towards PDUs?

Often, yes, if the content relates to project management and fits a Talent Triangle area. Record the provider, title, date and hours, and check PMI's guidance on qualifying activities.
