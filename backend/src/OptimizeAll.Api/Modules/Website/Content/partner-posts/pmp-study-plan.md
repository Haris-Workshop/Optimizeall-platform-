Most people preparing for the PMP have a job, a family and a calendar that is already full. A study plan that assumes four free hours every evening will be abandoned by week two. What works is a plan built around realistic time, clear weekly goals and plenty of practice with exam-style questions.

This **PMP study plan** spreads preparation over eight weeks at roughly eight to ten hours a week. If you have less time, stretch it to twelve weeks; if you have more, you may compress it. Either way, keep the structure: learn, practise, review, test.

Before you start, check two things on PMI's website: that you meet the current eligibility requirements, and which version of the exam content outline applies on your exam date. PMI introduced an updated PMP exam in July 2026, so make sure your materials match the outline you will be tested on. Our guide to the [PMP exam changes in 2026](/blog/pmp-exam-changes-2026) explains what to look for.

If anything in this plan conflicts with the official requirements published by PMI, follow PMI.

## How the plan is built

Three principles shape every week:

1. **The exam content outline is the syllabus.** PMI's outline lists the domains and tasks the exam tests. Study materials are a means to cover it, not a replacement for it.
2. **Practice questions drive learning.** Reading builds familiarity; answering scenario questions and reviewing every explanation builds judgement. Expect to spend at least half your time on questions from week 3 onward.
3. **Spaced review beats cramming.** Short, repeated reviews of earlier topics keep them fresh. Our guide to [spaced repetition and active recall](/blog/spaced-repetition-and-active-recall-for-exams) explains why.

## A typical week

| Day | Time | Activity |
|---|---|---|
| Monday | 60 min | Learn new topic (reading or video) |
| Tuesday | 60 min | Practice questions on Monday's topic, review explanations |
| Wednesday | 60 min | Learn second topic |
| Thursday | 60 min | Practice questions, review, update error log |
| Friday | 30 min | Spaced review: flashcards and error log |
| Saturday | 2–3 hours | Mixed practice set under time pressure, deep review |
| Sunday | Rest or 30 min light review | |

Adjust days to fit your life. The pattern matters more than the specific days.

## The eight weeks

### Week 1: Orientation and diagnostic

- Read PMI's exam content outline end to end. Note the domains and the approach mix (predictive, agile and hybrid).
- Take a short diagnostic quiz without studying. Its purpose is to reveal gaps, not to score well.
- Set up an **error log**: a simple table recording each question you miss, the topic, why you missed it and the principle you should have applied.
- Fix your study schedule in your calendar.

### Week 2: Foundations of project delivery

- Principles of project management, value delivery and the project environment.
- Development approaches and life cycles: when predictive, agile or hybrid fits.
- Start practice questions on these topics: 15–20 per session.

### Week 3: People

- Team leadership, servant leadership, conflict, motivation, team performance and stakeholder engagement.
- Practise situational questions: what should the project manager do *first*?
- Target: 100 practice questions this week with full review.

### Week 4: Process — planning and delivery

- Scope, schedule, cost, quality, resources, procurement, risk and communications in predictive, agile and hybrid contexts.
- Know the core calculations — for example earned value, critical path and float — and, more importantly, how to interpret them.
- Target: 120 practice questions.

### Week 5: Process — agile and hybrid in depth

- Backlogs, iterations, flow, retrospectives, value-driven prioritisation and adaptive planning.
- Many candidates underestimate how much agile and hybrid thinking runs through the exam. Our guide to [PMP agile situational questions](/blog/pmp-agile-situational-questions) gives worked examples.
- Target: 120 practice questions.

### Week 6: Business environment and integration

- Strategy alignment, benefits, compliance, governance, organisational change and the wider business context.
- Revisit your weakest topics from the error log.
- Take your **first full-length mock exam** under exam conditions. Review it over the following two days.

### Week 7: Targeted repair

- Use mock results to rank topics by weakness.
- Spend 70% of study time on the bottom three topics.
- Second full-length mock at the end of the week.

### Week 8: Consolidation and exam readiness

- Light new learning only; focus on review and timed mixed sets.
- Final mock early in the week, not the day before the exam.
- Prepare logistics: ID, test environment, timing plan. Our [PMP exam day checklist](/blog/pmp-exam-day-checklist) covers the details.

## Adapting the plan to your time

**If you have only four to six weeks.** Combine weeks 2 and 3 and weeks 4 and 5, increase weekday study to around 90 minutes, and take your first full mock at the end of week 3. Be realistic: a compressed plan works best for people with strong, recent project experience across predictive and agile approaches.

**If you have twelve weeks.** Keep the same sequence but give each content week a week and a half, add a third full mock in week 10, and use the extra time for spaced review of earlier topics. This pace suits people with demanding jobs or young families.

**If life interrupts.** Illness, a project crisis or a family event can wipe out a week. Do not try to catch up by doubling the next week. Push the plan back by a week, protect the mocks and the final consolidation week, and move your exam date if needed.

## How to review a practice question properly

The review is where most learning happens. For each question — including those you got right by guessing — ask:

1. What was the question really asking? (Often "what should you do **first**?" or "what is the **best** response?")
2. Which answer best reflects PMI's mindset: proactive, collaborative, value-focused and following agreed processes?
3. Why are the other options worse, not just why is the right one right?
4. What principle will I apply next time?

Write the principle in your error log. Over eight weeks, the log becomes your most valuable revision document. Our guide on [how to review a mock exam](/blog/how-to-review-a-mock-exam) goes deeper.

## How many practice questions?

There is no magic number, but most successful candidates answer several hundred to over a thousand questions and, crucially, review every one. Quality of review matters more than quantity. Our guide to [PMP practice questions](/blog/pmp-practice-questions) explains how to use a question bank well.

## Knowing when you are ready

Readiness is about consistency, not a single good score. Signs you are ready include stable mock exam results that you are comfortable with across several attempts, no domain where you are consistently weak, and the ability to finish full-length mocks within the time limit without rushing at the end. ## Frequently asked questions

### Is eight weeks enough to prepare for the PMP?

For many working professionals with project experience, eight to twelve weeks of steady study is realistic. If your experience is mainly predictive and the exam's agile and hybrid content is new to you, allow more time.

### How many hours a day should I study for the PMP?

About one hour on weekdays and two to three hours on one weekend day works for many people. Consistency matters more than long sessions.

### Do I need to read the whole PMBOK Guide?

PMI's exam content outline is the syllabus, and the exam draws on a range of references. Use the PMBOK Guide and other materials to understand concepts, but focus your time on the outline and on practice questions.

### When should I take my first full mock exam?

Around the middle of your preparation, once you have covered most topics. Earlier mocks are discouraging; later ones leave too little time to fix gaps.
