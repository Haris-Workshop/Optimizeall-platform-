Professionals who work on projects but are not sure whether they are "project managers" or "project controls people" often end up comparing two credentials: PMI's **PMP** (Project Management Professional) and PCI AI's **PCL-AI** (Project Controls Leader). On the surface they overlap — both mention schedules, cost and risk. Underneath, they answer different questions about your role.

This guide compares **PMP vs PCL-AI**: what each covers, who each suits, how the exams differ, how the two complement each other and how to decide which to pursue first. Details come from each body's public information; requirements change, so confirm them with PMI and PCI AI before you commit.

## The core difference

- The **PMP** certifies project **management**: leading people, engaging stakeholders, choosing delivery approaches, managing scope, schedule, cost, risk and quality, and aligning projects with business value, across predictive, agile and hybrid ways of working.
- The **PCL-AI** certifies project **controls**: the integrated discipline of planning, scheduling, cost engineering, earned value, forecasting, risk and project finance, with the governed use of AI built into each part, according to PCI AI.

Put simply, project managers decide and lead; project controls professionals measure, forecast and give decision-makers an honest picture. Our guide to [project controls vs project management](/blog/project-controls-vs-project-management) explores the distinction in depth.

## Side-by-side comparison

| | PMP | PCL-AI |
|---|---|---|
| Awarding body | Project Management Institute (PMI) | PCI AI |
| Focus | Project management and leadership across predictive, agile and hybrid delivery | Integrated project controls: planning, scheduling, cost, earned value, forecasting, risk, project finance, governed AI |
| Eligibility | Experience leading projects plus 35 hours of project management education (or a current CAPM), with the experience required depending on education | Open to anyone with three years' experience, in any field, according to PCI AI |
| Exam format | Computer-based, at a test centre or online; situational questions across the People, Process and Business Environment domains | Fully online, scenario-based, 90 minutes, 65% to pass, according to PCI AI |
| Fee and validity | Set by PMI; maintained with 60 PDUs every three years | USD 350 exam fee; valid for three years, according to PCI AI |

Check PMI's handbook and PCI AI's site for the current details. Our guides to [PMP eligibility requirements](/blog/pmp-eligibility-requirements) and the [PCL-AI certification](/blog/what-is-pcl-ai-certification) go deeper on each.

## What each exam feels like

### PMP

PMP questions typically describe a situation — a team conflict, a stakeholder demand, a slipping iteration — and ask what the project manager should do, often first. The best answers reflect PMI's mindset: proactive, collaborative, value-focused and respectful of agreed processes. Calculations exist but are a small part; judgement about people and process dominates. PMI updated the exam content outline in July 2026; see our guide to the [PMP exam changes in 2026](/blog/pmp-exam-changes-2026).

### PCL-AI

PCI AI describes its exams as scenario-based. Based on the scope it publishes, expect scenarios where you interpret controls data — earned value indices, forecasts, schedule quality, risk ranges, funding implications — and choose the best analysis, communication or action, including how AI tools should be governed. PCI AI publishes sample questions, which are the best guide to the style; our [PCI AI exam preparation guide](/blog/how-to-prepare-for-pci-ai-exams) sets out a study plan.

## Who should choose which

### Choose the PMP first if

- you lead projects and teams, or want to;
- your target roles are project manager, programme manager or delivery lead;
- employers in your market ask for the PMP;
- you meet PMI's experience requirement.

### Choose the PCL-AI first if

- you work in planning, scheduling, cost control, estimating or PMO analysis;
- you want to lead a controls function or become the person who owns forecasts;
- you want a credential that tests governed AI use within controls work;
- you have three years' experience in any field and want an integrated controls credential.

### Consider both if

- you are a project manager who wants to challenge controls reports with confidence; or
- you are a controls professional moving towards project leadership.

The credentials complement each other: the PMP gives breadth in leadership and delivery; the PCL-AI gives depth in the numbers that underpin decisions.

## A worked career example

**Elena** is a senior planner on infrastructure projects. She builds and updates schedules, runs schedule risk analysis and contributes to monthly reports. Her goal is to become head of project controls. The PCL-AI matches her direction: it tests the integrated controls discipline she will need to lead. Later, a PMP could help her work more effectively with project directors.

**Marcus** is an IT project manager who leads implementations and manages vendors. His CFO increasingly questions his forecasts. The PMP fits his core role, and he may later add controls depth through a credential like the PCL-AI or through focused learning in earned value and forecasting.

## Where the skills overlap and where they differ

| Skill area | PMP emphasis | PCL-AI emphasis (per PCI AI's published scope) |
|---|---|---|
| Scheduling | Planning the work and managing the schedule as part of delivery | Building, assessing and forecasting with logic-driven schedules |
| Cost | Budgeting and controlling cost within the project | Cost engineering, earned value and forecasting at completion |
| Risk | Identifying and responding to risks with the team | Quantifying risk, ranges and contingency for forecasts |
| People and leadership | Central: teams, stakeholders, conflict, servant leadership | Communicating analysis and influencing decisions |
| Delivery approaches | Predictive, agile and hybrid ways of working | Controls that work across approaches |
| Finance | Business value and benefits | Project finance and funding implications |
| AI | Responsible use within project management | Governed AI built into every controls discipline |

Reading across the table shows why the two credentials complement each other rather than compete: one leads the work, the other measures and forecasts it.

## Maintaining both

If you hold both, plan maintenance together. The PMP requires 60 PDUs every three years; PCI AI publishes its own recertification requirements on its site. ## A quick self-assessment

Score yourself from 1 (rarely) to 5 (daily) on each statement:

- I lead people and make delivery decisions.
- I build or assess schedules and cost forecasts.
- I present performance data and forecasts to sponsors.
- I negotiate with stakeholders about scope and priorities.
- I am asked to explain why the numbers moved.

If your highest scores are on leading people, negotiating and making delivery decisions, start with the PMP. If they are on schedules, forecasts and explaining numbers, start with the PCL-AI. If both are high, you are probably moving between the roles, and both credentials will be useful over time.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is the PCL-AI a replacement for the PMP?

No. They certify different disciplines. The PMP covers project management and leadership; the PCL-AI covers integrated project controls with governed AI. Many professionals benefit from both.

### Do I need the PMP before the PCL-AI?

No. PCI AI describes PCL-AI as open to anyone with three years' experience in any field. Check PCI AI's eligibility page for the full requirements.

### Which is better for project controls jobs?

For roles centred on planning, scheduling, cost and forecasting, an integrated controls credential such as the PCL-AI aligns more closely with the work. Check job adverts in your market to see what employers ask for.

### Does PCI AI have a project management credential?

Yes. PCI AI also offers the PML-AI (Project Management Leader – AI) credential covering governance, planning, execution, agile and hybrid delivery and AI-enabled project management. See our [PML-AI guide](/blog/pml-ai-project-management-leader-certification).
