Before you buy a single study guide, make sure you can actually sit the exam. The PMP has eligibility requirements that combine project management experience and project management education, and the application asks you to describe your experience in a specific way. Getting this right early saves weeks of frustration.

This guide explains the **PMP eligibility requirements** as PMI has published them, how to count your experience, how to earn and document the education hours, and how to write project descriptions that stand up if your application is selected for audit. PMI can change its requirements, so always confirm the current details in PMI's certification handbook before you apply.

In short: read the official requirements in PMI's handbook first, then use this guide to prepare your application.

## The requirements at a glance

PMI's published requirements combine three elements: your education level, months of experience leading projects, and hours of project management education. As published by PMI at the time of writing:

| Your education | Experience leading projects | Project management education |
|---|---|---|
| Four-year degree (bachelor's or global equivalent) | 36 months within the past eight years | 35 contact hours, or a current CAPM |
| High school diploma or associate's degree (or global equivalent) | 60 months within the past eight years | 35 contact hours, or a current CAPM |
| Bachelor's or postgraduate degree from a programme accredited by PMI's Global Accreditation Center | 24 months within the past eight years | 35 contact hours, or a current CAPM |

If you hold a current CAPM, PMI has accepted it in place of the 35 contact hours. Check whether that still applies and whether your CAPM is current on the date you apply.

## What counts as "leading projects"

You do not need the job title "project manager". PMI is interested in whether you **led and directed** project work. That can include roles such as project coordinator, team lead, engineer leading a work package, analyst running an implementation or consultant managing a client engagement — as long as you were responsible for leading part or all of a project.

Useful tests:

- **Was it a project?** A temporary endeavour with a defined objective and an end, rather than ongoing operations.
- **Did you lead?** You planned, coordinated, made decisions, managed stakeholders or directed the work of others, rather than only carrying out tasks.
- **Is it within the window?** Experience must fall within the period PMI specifies (eight years at the time of writing).

### How months are counted

Experience is counted in months, and overlapping projects in the same month do not count twice. If you led two projects at the same time from January to June, that is six months, not twelve. Keep a simple spreadsheet listing each project, start and end month, your role and the months that count.

## The 35 contact hours

The 35 hours must be project management education or training. Common sources include:

- courses from PMI Authorized Training Partners;
- university or college courses on project management;
- employer training programmes on project management;
- online courses that provide a record of completion and hours.

Keep certificates or completion records showing the provider, the course title, the dates and the hours. Courses on general topics such as spreadsheet skills or unrelated technical subjects usually do not count unless their content is project management.

## A worked example of counting months

Priya has a bachelor's degree. Over the past eight years she has:

- led a website migration from January 2019 to December 2019 (12 months);
- led a CRM rollout from October 2019 to September 2020 (12 months), overlapping the migration for three months;
- worked in operations support from October 2020 to March 2022 (not project work);
- led three process-improvement projects back to back from April 2022 to December 2023 (21 months).

Counting months once, regardless of overlap: January 2019 to September 2020 is 21 months, and April 2022 to December 2023 is another 21 months, giving 42 months of experience leading projects. The operations support period does not count. Under the requirement published at the time of writing for degree holders, 42 months exceeds the 36 needed, provided all of it falls within the eight-year window on the date she applies.

Keeping a spreadsheet like this before you start the application makes the online form much faster and reduces the risk of errors an auditor might question.

## Writing project descriptions

The application asks for a summary of each project you are counting. These descriptions matter: they are what an auditor reads. A clear structure helps:

1. **Objective** — what the project set out to achieve.
2. **Your role** — your title and what you were responsible for.
3. **Responsibilities and deliverables** — what you actually did across initiating, planning, executing, monitoring and closing (or across the equivalent agile activities).
4. **Outcome** — what was delivered.

### Example

> *Objective:* Replace the warehouse management system at two distribution centres within nine months. *Role:* Project lead. *Responsibilities:* Defined scope with operations managers, built and maintained the schedule and budget, coordinated a team of six internal staff and two vendors, ran fortnightly steering meetings, managed risks and change requests, and led cut-over planning. *Outcome:* System live at both sites on schedule, with a planned two-week stabilisation period.

Keep descriptions factual and specific. Avoid copying the same text for every project; auditors notice.

## Preparing for a possible audit

PMI selects a percentage of applications for audit. If yours is chosen, you will typically be asked to provide evidence such as copies of your education credentials, certificates for your contact hours and signatures from supervisors or managers confirming your project experience. Practical steps:

- Tell former supervisors in advance that you may ask them to confirm your experience.
- Keep contact details current.
- Keep all certificates in one folder before you submit.
- Make sure your descriptions match what supervisors will confirm.

An audit is not an accusation; it is routine. Being prepared turns it into a short administrative step.

## Common mistakes

- **Counting overlapping months twice.** Months are counted once, however many projects ran.
- **Counting operational work.** Ongoing support, business-as-usual tasks or routine maintenance are not projects.
- **Vague descriptions.** "Managed various projects" tells an auditor nothing.
- **Missing training evidence.** If you cannot document the 35 hours, they may not be accepted.
- **Ignoring the time window.** Experience older than the stated window does not count.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need a project manager job title to qualify for the PMP?

No. PMI looks at whether you led and directed project work, not your job title. Describe your leadership responsibilities clearly in the application.

### Can I count agile project experience?

Yes. Experience leading agile or hybrid projects counts as project experience. Describe your responsibilities in terms of the work you led.

### Does a CAPM replace the 35 contact hours?

PMI has accepted a current CAPM in place of the 35 hours. Confirm the current rule in PMI's handbook before applying.

### What happens if my application is audited?

You provide supporting documents, such as education credentials, training certificates and supervisor confirmations of your experience. Once the audit is complete, you can schedule your exam.

### How long does the PMP application take to complete?

If you have prepared a spreadsheet of projects, months and training records in advance, many people complete the online form in a few sessions. The time-consuming part is writing clear project descriptions, so draft them first.
