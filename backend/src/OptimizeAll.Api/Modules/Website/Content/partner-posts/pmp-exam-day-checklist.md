After weeks of preparation, the last thing you want is to lose focus on **PMP exam day** because your ID name does not match your registration, your webcam fails a system check or you run out of time on the final section. Most exam-day problems are avoidable with a little planning.

This checklist covers the week before, the night before, the morning of the exam and the exam itself, for both online-proctored and test-centre candidates. Rules on identification, breaks, permitted items and the testing environment are set by PMI and its testing provider and can change, so read the current PMI handbook and your appointment confirmation carefully. Where this guide and the official rules differ, the official rules apply.

Before working through the list, re-read the official requirements in your appointment confirmation; they take precedence over any checklist.

## One week before

### Logistics

- **Confirm your appointment** — date, time, time zone and delivery method (online or test centre).
- **Check your ID.** The name on your identification must match the name on your exam registration exactly as the rules require. If it does not, contact PMI or the testing provider well in advance.
- **Read the candidate rules** for your delivery method: permitted items, break rules, what happens if you are late and how to reschedule.

### Online-proctored candidates

- **Run the official system test** on the exact computer, network and room you will use. Do it again a day or two before the exam.
- **Prepare the room.** A quiet, private space with a clear desk and nothing on the walls within view that might be questioned. Plan for no interruptions.
- **Check your network.** A wired connection is often more stable than Wi-Fi. Ask others in the house not to stream video during your exam.
- **Close and disable** software that may interfere, such as automatic updates, notifications and background sync.

### Test-centre candidates

- **Plan the journey.** Know the route, parking or public transport, and how long it takes at that time of day. Consider a trial run.
- **Know the arrival time.** Test centres usually ask you to arrive early for check-in.

### Study

- Take your final full-length mock early in the week, not the day before.
- Shift to light review: your error log, key principles and formulas you tend to forget.
- Protect your sleep. Fatigue costs more marks than one more evening of revision gains.

## The night before

- Lay out your ID and anything permitted.
- Online: set up the room, charge devices, restart the computer, run the system test once more.
- Test centre: check travel updates and set two alarms.
- Do a short, calm review — 30 to 45 minutes at most — then stop.
- Eat normally and get to bed at your usual time.

## The morning of the exam

- Eat a familiar meal; avoid trying anything new.
- Online: log in early as instructed, with your ID ready, and expect a check-in process that includes photos of your workspace.
- Test centre: arrive with time to spare; expect identity checks and storage of personal belongings.
- Use the waiting time for slow breathing rather than last-minute cramming. Our guide to [exam anxiety](/blog/exam-anxiety-strategies) includes a simple breathing technique.

## During the exam

### Pacing

The PMP is a long exam, and pacing is a skill. Before exam day, divide the total time by the number of questions in your appointment details to get a target time per question, and set checkpoints — for example, where you should be at each quarter of the exam. Practise these checkpoints in your mocks. Our guide to [exam time management](/blog/exam-time-management-strategies) explains the method.

### Reading questions

- Read the last sentence first to see what is being asked: *first*, *next*, *best*, *most likely*.
- Identify the context: predictive, agile or hybrid; which phase; who is involved.
- Eliminate clearly wrong options, then compare the remaining ones against PMI's mindset: proactive, collaborative, value-focused, following agreed processes.

### Flagging

Flag questions you are unsure about and move on rather than spending several minutes on one item. Return to flagged questions at the end of the section if time allows. Check the current rules on whether you can return to questions after a break; on recent formats, reviewing has been limited to the section you are in.

### Breaks

Use scheduled breaks if they are offered. Stand, stretch, drink water and reset. Do not use breaks to review notes if that is not permitted, and follow the rules about leaving your desk for online exams strictly.

### If something goes wrong

- **Technical problem online:** use the proctor chat or follow the provider's instructions. Do not leave the camera view unless instructed.
- **Disruption at a test centre:** raise your hand and tell the administrator.
- **A run of hard questions:** expect it. Difficulty feels uneven; it does not mean you are failing. Take one slow breath and read the next question carefully.

## A pacing plan you can rehearse

Pacing is easier when you decide it before the exam rather than during it. A simple method that works for any long computer-based exam:

1. **Find the numbers.** Take the total exam time and the number of questions from your official appointment information.
2. **Calculate a target per question,** leaving a small reserve for reviewing flagged items — for example, plan to finish each section with a few minutes to spare.
3. **Set checkpoints.** Write down where you should be at each quarter of each section, in question numbers and minutes remaining.
4. **Rehearse in mocks.** Use exactly the same checkpoints in at least two full-length practice exams. Adjust if you are consistently early or late.
5. **Have a rule for hard questions.** For example: if you have no clear answer after a set time, eliminate what you can, choose the best remaining option, flag it and move on.

On the day, glance at the clock only at checkpoints. Constant clock-watching wastes time and raises anxiety.

## Food, water and comfort

Long exams are physical as well as mental. Eat a balanced meal a couple of hours before the start, drink enough water to stay comfortable without needing an unscheduled break, and wear layers so you can adjust to the room temperature. At a test centre, you may not be able to bring a jumper into the room once seated, so dress for comfort from the start. Online, make sure your chair and screen position are comfortable for several hours.

## After the exam

You will usually see a provisional result shortly after finishing, followed by an official notification. Whatever the outcome, note what felt hard while it is fresh. ## Frequently asked questions

### Should I take the PMP online or at a test centre?

Both are valid. Choose online if you have a quiet, private room and reliable internet, and are comfortable with remote proctoring rules. Choose a test centre if your home environment is unpredictable or you prefer a controlled setting.

### What ID do I need for the PMP exam?

Requirements are set by PMI and its testing provider and can change. Check the current handbook and your confirmation email, and make sure the name matches your registration exactly.

### Can I bring a calculator or notes?

Rules on permitted items differ by delivery method and are set by the testing provider. Typically, personal notes are not allowed. Read the current candidate rules for your appointment.

### What should I do the day before the PMP?

Light review only, logistics checks, normal meals and a normal bedtime. Avoid a full mock exam or late-night cramming.

### What if I feel ill on exam day?

Check the rescheduling and cancellation rules in your appointment confirmation and PMI handbook as early as possible. Sitting an exam while unwell rarely goes well, and the official rules explain your options.
