Some things are better on paper. A cheat sheet pinned next to your monitor, a checklist you tick off in a schedule review, a scorecard you fill in by hand each Sunday before deciding whether to book an exam. We have turned the most practical parts of our project controls and certification guides into seven free, printable PDFs.

Each guide is short — usually two to four pages — and designed to be used, not just read. Each one links back to the full article that explains the reasoning, so you can go deeper whenever you need to. They are free to download, print and share with colleagues; please keep the attribution and link back to this page if you share them more widely.

This page describes what each guide contains, who it is for and how to get the most from it.

## Project controls guides

These three guides support planners, schedulers, cost engineers, controls leads and project managers who want reliable numbers and governed use of AI.

### 1. Earned value management cheat sheet

**Download:** [Earned value management cheat sheet (PDF)](/downloads/earned-value-management-cheat-sheet.pdf)

**What is inside:**

- the core values — planned value, earned value, actual cost, budget at completion — with plain-language definitions;
- variances and indices (CV, SV, CPI, SPI) with how to read them;
- the main estimate at completion methods and the to-complete performance index, with when each fits;
- a summary of progress measurement methods (rules of credit);
- a five-question health check to run before you trust the numbers;
- a small worked example.

**Who it is for:** anyone who reads or produces earned value reports, from new analysts to sponsors who want to challenge a forecast.

**How to use it:** keep it beside you during the monthly reporting cycle. Before presenting CPI or SPI, run the health check questions. The full explanation is in [earned value management explained](/blog/earned-value-management-explained), with formulas compared in [estimate at completion formulas](/blog/estimate-at-completion-formulas).

### 2. Schedule health check template

**Download:** [Schedule health check template (PDF)](/downloads/schedule-health-check-template.pdf)

**What is inside:**

- the fourteen checks of the DCMA 14-point assessment with the thresholds commonly quoted;
- columns for your result, pass or fail, and a justification for accepted exceptions;
- a six-step review workflow from freezing a copy to tracking trends;
- a sign-off block for the planner and reviewer.

**Who it is for:** planners preparing a baseline or monthly update, and reviewers assessing contractor schedules.

**How to use it:** fill it in at each update and keep the completed sheets. Trends over several months tell you more than one result. Read the [DCMA 14-point assessment guide](/blog/dcma-14-point-schedule-assessment) for what each failure usually means and how to fix it.

### 3. AI in project controls: practical playbook

**Download:** [AI in project controls playbook (PDF)](/downloads/ai-in-project-controls-playbook.pdf)

**How to use it:** pick one use case, run the 30-day pilot and use the governance checklist before any wider rollout. ## Printing and using the guides

The PDFs are designed for A4 and US Letter paper and print well in black and white. A few tips:

- **Print the checklists, keep the cheat sheets digital too.** Tick-box guides work best on paper; reference sheets are handy on a second screen.
- **Use one copy per cycle.** For the schedule health check and the readiness scorecard, print a fresh copy each month or week so you can compare results over time.
- **Share them with your team.** The schedule health check and AI playbook are useful as common standards for a controls team; agree any changes to thresholds or review rules together.
- **Link rather than re-upload.** If you mention a guide in a newsletter, intranet or forum, link to this page so readers always get the latest version.

## A note on accuracy

The guides summarise widely used practices and the public information of the bodies concerned at the time of writing. Exam rules, fees and formats change, so always confirm details with the official awarding body — for example PMI for the PMP, and [PCI AI](https://pciai.org) for the PCL-AI, PFL-AI and PML-AI. Commonly quoted thresholds, such as those in the schedule health check, may differ from the ones in your contract.
